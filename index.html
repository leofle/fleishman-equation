The Fleishman Equation

An estimate of the chance that AI wipes out humanity, split into a rogue-AI path and a human-misuse path.

Read the white paper (PDF)

P(doom) = fT · [1 − (1 − R) · (1 − S)]
R = fM · fP · fU · fH · fX  (rogue path, given powerful AI exists)
S = fA · fW · fF  (misuse path, given powerful AI exists)
P(doom) is the probability that AI causes human extinction within a stated time frame. fT is counted once. Path contributions: P(rogue) = fT·R, P(misuse) = fT·S.

Assumptions

The two paths are treated as independent once powerful AI exists. In reality they can interact (for example, a misused AI could also escape control), so this is a simplification.

Path factors are implicitly averaged over when, within the time frame, powerful AI arrives; late arrival leaves little time for either path.

The calculator multiplies single values. If factors are uncertain, the product of their medians is in general not the median of the product.

The Grace et al. (2018) preset is conditional on HLMI existing (fT = 1), so it is not on the same scale as the other research presets. Per-factor values in total-only presets are artifacts of the equal-split mapping, not the respondents' forecasts.

Where safety work pushes the numbers

  • Alignment research lowers fM.
  • Oversight, interpretability, and shutdown mechanisms lower fU.
  • Compute governance and access controls lower fP and fA.
  • Biosecurity and AI-assisted defense lower fW and fF.

Like the Drake equation, the structure is the useful part; the inputs are guesses. Research presets reproduce the published totals; they do not mean the authors endorse our factor split. The URL updates as you move sliders, so you can share your numbers.
